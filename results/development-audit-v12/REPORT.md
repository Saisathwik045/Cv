## What the completed studies establish

- **v9:** on the reused 525-patient validation set, selected ConvNeXt cosine reached macro AUROC 0.84173 / AP 0.61131 / F1 0.59263, versus original ConvNeXt 0.82876 / 0.53982 / 0.47624. The original-versus-v9 contrast also changes checkpoint rules and budget; the within-v9 schedule contrast is cleaner. Neither independently confirms generalization.
- **v10:** containment-aware threshold CV raised macro F1 0.5926→0.6055, with FP 243→425 and FN 276→186. H remained TP3/FP1/FN13. It separates threshold fitting only; all validation labels already influenced the model. Do not deploy those thresholds.
- **v11:** paired training-only outer mean AUROC 0.83451→0.83549 (sqrt minus ratio +0.00098; conditional 95% interval −0.00930 to +0.01058), AP 0.57175→0.58350 and macro F1 0.53901→0.51359 (difference −0.02541; interval −0.04540 to −0.00435). The numerical AUROC nomination was not established improvement. Pooled FP fell 1440→1050 but FN rose 1127→1394. Different fit sizes/cohorts prevent a direct v9-versus-v11 performance trend.

- **H:** 42/67 positives have other labels, including D in 27; reference detects 4/42 multilabel versus 5/25 H-only patients. This is an association with small counts, not evidence that D causes errors. H has 67 positives across 66 components; limited support increases uncertainty but does not alone explain weak discrimination. Reference H false-negative score median is 0.0358 (IQR 0.0189–0.1420), square-root 0.1528. Only 6/58 and 3/58 misses respectively have one eye ≥.5 while the mean is below .5: mean-eye dilution cannot explain most H misses.
- **O:** 275/636 positives are multilabel (196 include D), while 361 are O-only. Reference detects 150/361 O-only and 143/275 multilabel positives. One-eye-positive/mean-negative occurs in 131/343 reference misses and 138/466 square-root misses. This identifies an aggregation/target-granularity tension; it does not validate max-eye aggregation or a new threshold.
- **Annotation semantics:** both eyes receive the same patient-derived eight-label vector in training; the loss therefore treats every positive patient label as an eye target even when source text differs by eye. Cached keywords cover 1,835/2,275 patients (440 absent), originating from duplicate-candidate selection, so this subset is biased. Of 528 covered O-positive patients, 259 mention “normal fundus” in exactly one eye, one in both, and there are 190 distinct eye-keyword strings. These literal strings demonstrate heterogeneity/localization ambiguity, not incorrect labels. For H, 54/60 covered positives mention “hypertensive retinopathy” in both eyes and six in one; simple unilateral-label inheritance cannot explain all H errors. No labels were remapped.
- **Preprocessing/quality:** recorded source geometry gives median short side 150px after resizing, median padding 33.0%, median long-side downscale 11.57×. All expected padding pixels match the frozen mean color. Code inspection confirms RGB/EXIF letterbox and matching ImageNet normalization; no preprocessing defect was identified in these checks. Native raw images are unavailable locally, so raw pixel parity, clinical gradability and fine anatomical detail remain unverified. Cached O contrast/gradient distributions overlap across errors and correct outcomes. H true-positive inputs have higher gradient-energy median (0.00688 versus 0.00161 for misses in reference), but texture, pathology, scale and source format confound this proxy; it is not a validated blur or quality explanation.

## Fixed technical visual review and uncertainty

The sampling protocol was committed at `c244d33` before selection/inspection. SHA256-ranked patient selection chose two distinct components per H/O × loss × TP/FP/FN/TN stratum: all **32 records, 18 unique pairs / 36 eyes** reviewed, five private montage pages. No score-extreme or appearance-based selection and no replacements/expansion. Correct predictions and errors both show padding, softness and illumination variation. Some O misses retain readable major structures. Small retinal discs/wide borders occur among both H true and false positives, suggesting a possible source-format shortcut to investigate, not proving device confounding. No clinical adjudication, diagnosis or annotation-error inference was made. Private case-by-case notes preserve every selected pair. These selected-case observations do not estimate quality prevalence.

**Hypotheses, not conclusions:** detail loss at 224px, source-format dependence, patient/eye supervision mismatch and heterogeneous O semantics may each contribute. Native images and source/clinical schema review are needed to distinguish them. Neither sparse H support, image quality, label noise, threshold choice nor unilateral disease has been established as the sole cause.

## Concrete recommendation

**Retain:** frozen demo/REVIEW REQUIRED, all five factories, cosine ratio-BCE development reference, both experimental losses and every result. **Reject:** claims that square-root weighting improved performance, automatic loss promotion, threshold deployment, sole-sample-size explanations, clinical relabeling from visual review and comparison with the historical holdout for tuning.

One next proposal is a matched **224 versus 320px ratio-BCE ConvNeXt study**, using the same three training-only outer/inner containment folds and fresh ImageNet weights. It tests spatial-detail retention without unverified cropping; it does not solve patient-derived label semantics. Estimate 4.23 worker-hours at all epoch ceilings, 5.50 hours with planning allowance; propose a new strict **six-hour free-quota / six-session cap**, including failures/resumes/overhead. No execution is authorized here. First recover/verify native TRAIN-only images and 224-cache parity, clarify H/O source definitions, freeze/test the new implementation and approve its exact budget. See the [complete settings, selection criteria and stopping rule](../../docs/NEXT_EXPERIMENT_AFTER_V12.md). If those prerequisites cannot be met, stop before training.

## Fresh independent evaluation feasibility

No new eligible cohort, access agreement, labels or overlap evidence has been established; confirmation is currently **not ready**. The used ODIR final holdout is historical, and unlabeled official-test images cannot confirm performance. Repartitioning this dataset is not a fresh independent evaluation. A prospective external-site or temporal cohort needs paired eyes, deidentified stable patient keys, acquisition/device/site metadata and documented lawful research access. Freeze enrollment and the complete new pipeline before evaluation.

A qualified labeling protocol must define all eight labels, especially H retinal findings versus systemic hypertension, heterogeneous O inclusion rules, exclusive N and patient-versus-eye semantics. Missing/unassessed labels must not become negatives. Independent readers and disagreement resolution, with eye-specific localization where feasible, are needed; existing keyword disagreement is not adjudication. Check overlap against **all** prior cohorts using steward-managed patient linkage and exact image hashes, then independent retinal-content evidence for suspected resized/recompressed copies. Do not recreate weak-hash giant chains. Handle uncertain overlaps prospectively; do not inspect old final outcomes for this task.

Choose sample size using class prevalence, desired interval precision, clustering and multiplicity. As an illustration only, roughly 97 independent positives per label gives worst-case binomial 95% recall half-width about .10 before design inflation; negatives are also needed. H recruitment may dominate feasibility. Current ODIR prevalence is not an external prevalence estimate. Rare-class enrichment needs prespecified sampling weights and cannot yield population PPV/calibration unweighted. There is no accuracy promise, clinical recall target or automatic acceptance policy.
